# Keystone: go-to-market plan

A working plan for getting the first 100, then 1,000 users. Written from what the app does today and what is already on the Play listing. Anything marked **Decide** needs your call before it can be done. Numbers I could not check are left out on purpose.

## 1. Pick the one person you are for (Decide)

The Play listing today says "calendar, goals, and people in one planner". That describes a hundred apps and gives nobody a reason to pick this one. What Keystone does that the big planners do not:

- a **people tracker** with reconnect nudges, follow-ups, a timeline per person, and a check-in after each visit
- a **map** of pins for the places and people you visit, with today's events on it
- all of it **offline first**, no ads, one-time Pro price

That points at one group: **people who spend their week visiting and keeping up with other people.** Choose one and write everything for them:

| Wedge | Why it fits | Where they gather |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Missionaries and mission leaders, ward and stake callings | Visits, follow-ups, a map of who lives where, a weekly review. The app grew from this. | Church-community Facebook groups, returned-missionary groups, Reddit r/lds, missionary-prep forums |
| Relationship-driven work (sales reps, real estate, recruiters, pastors, care visitors) | Same features: last contact, next follow-up, route plan | LinkedIn, r/sales, r/realtors, local meetups |
| Anyone "bad at staying in touch" | Largest group, hardest to reach, weakest reason to pay | TikTok / Instagram |

**Recommendation:** start with the first row if that is who you built it for, and keep the listing honest about it. A narrow group you can reach by name beats a wide group you reach by accident.

## 2. Fix the Play Store listing first

It is the one thing every visitor sees, and it costs nothing.

1. **Short description** (80 max). Lead with the person, not the features. Example once you pick a wedge: `Keep up with the people you visit: nudges, follow-ups, a map, and your week.`
2. **First three lines of the full description** are all most people read. Put the wedge and the one-sentence promise there, then the feature bullets.
3. **Screenshot order.** The first two decide the install. Lead with the People/timeline screen and the Home screen, not Settings or an empty calendar. Add a one-line caption on each image. Replace screenshot 06 (illustrated map) with a real one.
4. **Title keywords.** The title and short description are the strongest search signals. Use the words people type: "contact tracker", "follow-up reminders", "visit planner", "people planner". Check what the Play search box suggests as you type each one.
5. **Ratings.** Ask for a rating only after something went right (a streak, a finished week), never on launch. The app already has a "Rate on Google Play" link in Settings.

## 3. Get the first 100 (weeks 1 to 4)

You are on closed testing, so first:

- **Closed-test requirement.** A new personal developer account needs a closed test with enough testers for the required period before production. Check the current number and days in Play Console. Recruit to that number first; nothing else matters until it clears.
- **Where to find testers:** the communities in your wedge row, friends and family, one post in each group. Ask for feedback, not downloads. Offer to fix what they find the same week; fast replies turn testers into advocates.
- **A feedback loop:** the app already has "Send feedback". Reply to every message within a day.

After production:

| Channel | What to post | Effort |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Community groups (Facebook, Reddit) | A short, honest "I built this for myself, here is what it does" post with two screenshots. Follow each group's self-promotion rules. | Low |
| Short video (TikTok, Reels, Shorts) | 20-second screen recordings: "how I never forget to follow up", "my week in one screen" | Medium |
| A one-page site | What it is, three screenshots, the Play badge, the privacy policy. You already need a privacy policy URL. | Low |
| Word of mouth | The accountability-partner feature is built to be shared. Mention it in the listing and the first-run tour. | Built in |

## 4. Launch checklist

- [ ] Wedge chosen and used in the listing, site and posts
- [ ] Listing updated (short description, first three lines, screenshot order and captions)
- [ ] Privacy policy URL live and matching the data-safety form
- [ ] Closed-test requirement met; production access requested
- [ ] Redeploy done on Render; production Clerk keys in place; test a purchase with a licence tester
- [ ] A way to reach you that you will actually check
- [ ] One real screenshot of every main screen on a real phone

## 5. First 30 days of posts

One idea a day is too much. Aim for **three a week**:

- **Week 1: the problem.** "I forgot to follow up with someone again." Show the nudge. "What 'last contacted' tells you."
- **Week 2: the feature tour in ten seconds each.** The map, the weekly review, the check-in after an event, the home screen widget.
- **Week 3: proof.** A tester's quote (with permission), a before-and-after of a messy week, a milestone ("50 people using it").
- **Week 4: the offer.** Explain Pro in one honest sentence: one-time price, no subscription, everything added later included. People notice and remember this.

## 6. What to measure (keep it small)

Play Console shows all of this for free:

- **Store listing visitors → installs** (conversion). If it is low, fix the listing before anything else.
- **Day-1 and day-7 retention.** If people install and leave, more marketing only widens the leak.
- **Crashes and ANRs.** One bad crash on the first open undoes a week of posts.
- **Pro purchases** and the reviews that mention them.

## 7. What to avoid

- Paying for installs before retention is healthy.
- Claiming things the app does not do yet. Everything in the listing should be true on the build people download.
- Posting the same text in many groups. Rewrite it for each, and respect each group's rules on promotion.
